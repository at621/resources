Top-Down Multiyear Backtest of IFRS 9 ECL

Residential mortgages · the ECL booked at a reporting date vs. the loss later realised on the same loans · ECL level only, no component analysis
Main idea. At a reporting date t₀ the bank booked an ECL for every loan — a forecast of what those loans will lose, in today's money. The backtest keeps exactly those loans, waits h = 1 … 5 years, measures what they actually lost, discounts it back to t₀ the same way and compares the two sums. One cohort answers ① adequacy (was the provision enough?), five cohorts answer ② bias (wrong in the same direction every time?), a paired t-test answers ③ fit (is the gap more than noise?). Loans still on book at th are either left out on both sides (truncation test) or carried at the ECL they hold at th (roll-forward test) — panel A shows both.

AThe comparison — and the two ways to treat loans still on book

Truncation test — RL(h) vs EL(h); survivors left out on both sides
Roll-forward test — survivors carry ECL(th)
The two tests, definedTruncation (step 5): RL(h) ⋚ EL(h) = Σi Σk≤h mi,k  — both sides stop at year h: a survivor adds 0 to RL and only m₁…mh to EL. Tests the first h years of the term structure, nothing beyond.
Roll-forward (step 6): Σi Σk mi,k ⋚ RL(h) + Σsurvivors (1+ri)−h·ECLi(th)  — lifetime ECL at t₀ against realised loss plus the shadow lifetime ECL each survivor holds at th (best estimate of what is still to come). Tests the whole provision at every h; but the right side is the engine's own view — an optimistic engine passes its own roll-forward (h = 1 above) — so read it next to the truncation ratio, never alone.
  • ① Adequacy — was the provision enough? One cohort, one horizon: RL(h) against EL(h). The band is the bank's own plausible range at t₀ (upside-scenario ECL … downside-scenario ECL). Inside = adequate; above = worse than the stress case = under-provision (amber, red beyond 125 % of the downside); below = over-provision.
  • ② Bias — wrong in the same direction every time? One cohort sees one economy; one result can be luck. The 15 ratios ρ = RL/EL of all cohorts form the triangle. One red diagonal = one bad year. Red along rows, down columns and on both sides of it = bias (ECL systematically too low; blue = too high).
  • ③ Fit — is the gap more than noise? G = RL − EL is a sum of loan-level differences; ÷ its standard error = the paired t (panel E). Valid against the hindsight ECL only; |t| > 2 = mis-fit beyond chance even knowing the economy.
Symbols. i = a loan · mi,k = expected loss of loan i for development year k, exactly as the ECL engine computed it at t₀ (mi,1 = its 12-month ECL, Σk mi,k = its lifetime ECL) · RL(h) = Σi: τi≤h (1+ri)−τi·EADi(τi)·Li: τi year of default, ri EIR, EADi(τi) balance at default, Li realised LGD (panel F) · ECLi(th) = shadow lifetime ECL at th.

BProcedure

  1. Define the test
    Reference dates 31 Dec 2021 … 2025, cut-off 31 Dec 2026, portfolio. Same conventions both sides: default = 90 dpd / UTP; PV at t₀ at the loan's EIR; a loss is dated by its default year.
  2. Freeze one cohort per t₀
    Every loan on book with stage, EAD, EIR, booked ECL, upside / downside ECL and expected loss per year m₁ … mL. Never restated.
  3. Two populations
    (a) Stage 2 + 3 at their booked lifetime ECL; (b) whole book, Stage 1 at its shadow lifetime ECL. Booked ECL as is at h = 1.
  4. Realised loss per loan
    First default after t₀ (year τ), EAD then, cash discounted to τ → L; loss = (1+r)−τ·EAD·L. Cure = cost only; unresolved = cash so far + Stage 3 provision; Stage 3 at t₀: EAD − PV(cash after t₀).
  5. Truncation test, per horizon
    EL(h) = Σm₁…mh vs RL(h), h = 1 … H: ratio ρ, gap G, defaults, unresolved share (panel A left).
  6. Roll-forward test
    Lifetime ECL at t₀ vs RL(h) + PV of the shadow lifetime ECL held on survivors at th (panel A right). Report next to step 5.
  7. Hindsight ECL
    Same engine, same loans, realised macro path. RL − hindsight = the engine's fit; hindsight − booked = what the scenarios missed.
  8. Triangle
    Rows t₀, columns h, diagonals = calendar years. Same sign along rows and columns, on both sides of the stress diagonal = bias.
  9. Grade and escalate
    Green inside the upside–downside band; amber ≤ 25 % outside or unresolved > 25 %; red beyond. Amber → owner explains; red → committee.
  10. Paired t-test
    Per cell: di = realised − expected per loan, t = mean(d)/[sd(d)/√n]. Valid with hindsight ECL as expected, indicative with booked (panel E).
  11. Data checks, repeat yearly
    Default definition stable; forbearance, sales and unresolved shares reported per cell. Full triangle every year-end.

CStage migrations and write-off

Moves between Stage 1 and Stage 2 after t₀
  • EL(h) and RL(h) do not change: stage and expected loss per year are frozen at t₀; a loan that moves 1→2 or 2→1 and never defaults has RL = 0.
  • They matter in one place only: the roll-forward adds each survivor's shadow lifetime ECL at th, so a 2→1 move cannot flatter the test.
Cure and re-default
  • Cure = same default event; realised loss = workout cost K only. The ECL already prices the chance of a cure, so the expected side is cure-adjusted too.
  • Re-default inside probation = same event (cash flows continue); after probation = a second default event of the same loan, counted again — the unit of RL is the default event.
Write-off is not relevant to this test, because
  • (a) it is an accounting entry, not a default — the loss is recognised at τ and dated there;
  • (b) its timing is policy-driven, 1–4 years after default, so it would misdate and understate losses;
  • (c) cash can still arrive after it — only cash and costs enter L, whenever they occur.
The write-off date is therefore not used anywhere on this page.

Both tests read off this matrix. Truncation: EL = m₁…mh, RL as shown. Roll-forward: EL = the full lifetime Σk m, and in the "still on book" column RL = the survivor's shadow lifetime ECL at th discounted to t₀; all other columns identical. All transitions covered? Yes, by construction: every loan is in exactly one row (its stage at t₀) and one column at th (six exclusive, exhaustive end states); whatever happened in between only decides the column, so the cells sum to EL(h) and RL(h) exactly once. Conventions: second default after probation = second event; partial prepayment ≠ exit; EIR frozen at t₀; sold while in default = Stage 3 × sold; forbearance without default = still on book.

EThe t-test (step 10): how to use it on ECL and how to read it

Paired t-test, one per cohort × horizon celldi = realised lossi(h) − expected lossi(h) for every loan i of the cohort;  t = d̄ / [ sd / √n ], df = n − 1
H₀: mean difference = 0; one-sided alternative "realised > expected" = under-provision. Σdi = G(h): the test is the gap divided by its loan-level standard error. Loans that never default enter with di = −expected.
  • On the hindsight ECL — valid. With the economy held fixed, loan-level differences are independent; t tests whether the engine is biased for these loans. |t| > 2 ≈ 5 % two-sided; with 15 cells read the pattern, not one cell.
  • On the booked ECL — indicative only. All loans share one macro surprise, which the standard error ignores, so |t| is inflated in stress years; adequacy on the booked basis is graded by the band (step 9).
  • Not on the 15 cell ratios against 1: the cells overlap in loans and years; the effective sample is one or two.

FDefinitions
Static cohort
every loan on book at a reference date t₀ (31 Dec 2021 … 2025), frozen with the ECL it carried; loans leave only by prepayment, maturity, sale or default, none are added.
Default, τ, development year h
default = 90 days past due or unlikely to pay; year h = calendar year t₀ + h; τ = the h in which a loan defaults; a loss belongs to its default year.
Stage 1 / 2 / 3
1 = performing, 12-month ECL booked (= m₁); 2 = significant increase in credit risk, lifetime ECL booked (= Σmh); 3 = in default, ECL = EAD × in-default LGD.
Shadow lifetime ECL
lifetime ECL computed for a Stage 1 loan with the same engine — used in the test (and for survivors in the roll-forward), never booked.
Realised loss, realised LGD L
(1+r)−τ × EAD(τ) × L, with L = 1 − [Σj Rj(1+r)−(uj−τ) − K] / EAD(τ): Rj = cash received in year uj, K = workout cost. Zero for loans that never default.
Hindsight ECL
the same ECL engine run again at t₀ on the frozen loans with the macro path that actually happened — the forecast the bank would have booked had it known the economy.
